How We Mill Custom Caps for Brands: From Fabric Rolls to Finished Headwear

Published by Capsmill | Est. Reading Time: 8 min

If you are searching for a custom cap manufacturer or an OEM hat supplier, you have likely seen hundreds of factories claiming they can produce your design. However, there is a massive difference between a factory that assembles caps and one that mills them.

At Capsmill, we don't just sew logos onto blanks. We mill the cap from the ground up—starting with raw textiles and ending with a retail-ready product that fits your brand's exact silhouette, drape, and aesthetic. This guide walks you through the 6 critical stages of our milling process, so you understand exactly what happens inside our workshop before your samples ship to the USA.

1. Technical Consultation & Tech Pack Grading

The milling process begins long before any fabric is cut. Once we receive your tech pack (or a physical reference sample), our pattern engineers conduct a digital grading session. We analyze the crown depth, front panel rise (usually between 4.25" to 4.75" for structured caps), and the visor curvature radius.

We use 3D CAD software to map out the seam allowances and panel shrinkage rates. This step is critical because different fabrics (like cotton twill vs. polyester-spandex) behave differently under heat. Without this digital blueprint, the cap might shrink or warp after the first wash. This precision milling step ensures that your production run maintains a consistent fit across all sizes.

2. Fabric Selection & Interlining Fusion

Choosing the right materials is what separates a premium cap from a promotional giveaway. We maintain a vast inventory of 100% cotton twill (ranging from 210 to 290 GSM), washed cotton for a vintage hand-feel, and high-stretch performance polyester blends.

However, the real secret to our milling process lies in the interlining (buckram). We do not use generic fusible sheets. For structured caps, we fuse a high-density, non-woven buckram specifically onto the two front panels. We also offer a "soft" structured option using lightweight woven interfacing that provides shape without stiffness. The fusion process involves heat-pressing these layers at controlled temperatures to prevent delamination (bubbling) after months of wear.

3. Automated Die-Cutting & Panel Preparation

Once the fabric is backed and stabilized, we move to our automated cutting tables. Why is this important? Hand-cutting can result in a 2-3mm variation per panel. When you stitch a cap with 6 panels, a 2mm error on each seam adds up to a 12mm crooked crown.

Our cutting machines mill the fabric stacks with ±0.5mm tolerance. At this stage, we also prepare the mesh for the rear panels (typically 100% polyester performance mesh) and cut the visor inserts. We use a semi-rigid PE board for the brim that returns to its original shape even after being crushed in luggage—an essential feature for travel-friendly streetwear brands.

4. The Assembly Line: Where Craftsmanship Meets Machinery

This is the core of our cap milling operation. Our assembly line is divided into specialized stations:

  • Closing & Shirring: We stitch the 6 panels together using industrial Juki machines. For curved visor caps, we employ "shirring" (tightly curved stitching) to give the brim its natural crescent shape.
  • Visor Insertion: The PE board is inserted and stitched using a 6-needle pattern to ensure consistent stitch count across every cap.
  • Top Button & Eyelet Setting: We use hydraulic presses to set reinforced metal eyelets (6 or 8 hole options) and the top button, ensuring they are flush with the fabric to prevent snagging.
  • Custom Seam Taping: We apply custom-branded seam tape along the interior seams. This not only hides raw edges but also provides a sweat barrier, prolonging the cap's lifespan.

5. High-Definition Embroidery & 3D Puff Digitization

A cap is only as good as its logo execution. We house an on-site embroidery unit with multi-head Tajima machines.

We don't just run your logo through auto-digitization. Our digitizers manually map the stitch density, pull compensation, and underlay. For 3D puff embroidery, we apply a foam layer beneath the stitches, which elevates the logo by 4-5mm. For flat satin stitches, we adjust the angle of the thread to catch light dynamically.

Additionally, we mill the underbrim details—if your design requires a contrasting fabric or printed pattern under the visor, we apply that using a separate sublimation or fabric bonding process before the final assembly.

6. Rigorous Quality Control (The Final Inspection)

Before any cap leaves our mill, it undergoes a 3-stage QC inspection:

  • Measurement Check: We measure the crown height and brim width against your tech pack.
  • Stitch Density: We count the stitches per inch (SPI) to ensure they meet the required standard (typically 8-10 SPI).
  • Wash Test: We pull random samples and subject them to a wash test to check for shrinkage or color bleeding (especially crucial for 100% cotton washed twill).

We provide comprehensive QC photos and videos before packaging so you can approve the final aesthetics before we ship.
